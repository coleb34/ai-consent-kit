Rhode Island AI rules for therapists

What Rhode Island requires of mental health clinicians who use AI note-takers, scribes or other AI tools, with sources.

AI laws to know in Rhode Island

LawStatusEffectiveWhat it covers
H 7349 (P.L. 2026 ch. 373; R.I. Gen. Laws ch. 40.1-5.5), Oversight of AI Technology in Mental Health Care ActIn effectJune 22, 2026Bars offering therapy or psychotherapy services to the public, including through internet-based AI, unless a licensed professional conducts them, and limits how licensed professionals may use AI, with written disclosure and consent conditions.
S 2195 / H 7350 (AI chatbot safety, self-harm protocols)Enacted, not yet in effectJanuary 1, 2027Operators may not provide an AI companion without a protocol for suicidal ideation, self-harm or physical harm to others expressed by a user, including crisis referrals, and must notify users at the start and at least every three hours that they are not communicating with a human.
S 2570 (P.L. 2026 ch. 372; R.I. Gen. Laws ch. 23-108), Use of Artificial Intelligence by Healthcare Providers Notification ActIn effectJune 22, 2026Healthcare providers and healthcare facilities that employ AI to document in-person or telehealth visits must notify patients of that use and review the AI-generated documentation for accuracy after the visit.

What this means for your practice

  • Do not allow or use AI to directly interact with clients in any form of therapeutic communication without an established relationship in which you agree to undertake diagnosis and treatment of the patient and the patient consents under the section. (effective June 22, 2026) Source
  • Follow Rhode Island's Oversight of Artificial Intelligence Technology in Mental Health Care Act if you hold a valid license, credential or certification issued by the state to provide therapy or psychotherapy services. (effective June 22, 2026) Source
  • Get the consent to that use of AI from the patient or the patient's parent, guardian or other legally authorized representative. (effective June 22, 2026) Source
  • Give written information and get consent before AI designed to simulate emotional attachment, bonding or dependency, or an AI companion for mental health or emotional support, assists in supplementary support or therapeutic communication where the client's session is recorded or transcribed. (effective June 22, 2026) Source
  • If a client discloses using software with AI features on their own initiative, you may discuss and offer guidance about that use; you are responsible for maintaining confidentiality, monitoring the client's safety, intervening when necessary, and discussing the software's limitations and risks. (effective June 22, 2026) Source
  • If you are a healthcare professional licensed by the director of the Department of Health and you employ AI to document in-person or telehealth visits, notify patients of the use of AI for that sole purpose. (effective June 22, 2026) Source
  • In that written information, tell the patient or the patient's parent, guardian or other legally authorized representative that AI will be used and the specific purpose of the AI tool or system. (effective June 22, 2026) Source
  • Keep all records and all communications with individuals seeking therapy or psychotherapy services confidential, and disclose them only as provided under § 40.1-5-26. (effective June 22, 2026) Source
  • Keep responsibility for clinical judgment and reasonable therapeutic oversight of the patient's use of an AI system; the Act does not place responsibility on you for vendor-controlled system design, algorithms or outputs. (effective June 22, 2026) Source
  • Know the enforcement: the Executive Office of Health and Human Services may investigate any actual, alleged or suspected violation of the chapter, and the penalties under § 5-37.3-9 apply to violations of its confidentiality provisions. (effective June 22, 2026) Source
  • Know the exemptions: the chapter does not apply to religious counseling, peer support, public self-help and educational materials that do not purport to offer therapy, AI tools reviewed and cleared by the FDA or another federal agency, or approved research. (effective June 22, 2026) Source
  • Make sure consent is an affirmative written agreement (electronic is allowed) that is explicit, express, freely given, informed, voluntary, specific and revocable; accepting general or broad terms of use that describe AI along with unrelated information is not consent. (effective June 22, 2026) Source
  • Review AI-generated documentation of in-person or telehealth visits for accuracy after the visit. (effective June 22, 2026) Source
  • Use AI only to the extent the use meets the requirements of subsection (a) of the Act's use-restrictions section (written information and consent), and do not allow or use AI to make independent therapeutic decisions or to determine therapeutic recommendations or treatment plans. (effective June 22, 2026) Source

Good practice everywhere, whatever your state requires

  • Written consent from every adult participant before recording or transcribing a session.
  • A HIPAA business associate agreement for any AI tool that receives client information.
  • A clinician reviews every AI-assisted note.
  • AI never delivers therapy or makes clinical decisions.
