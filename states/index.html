CaliforniaAB 3030, generative AI in patient communications (Health & Safety Code § 1339.75)In effectJanuary 1, 2025Health facilities, clinics and physician offices that use generative AI to communicate patient clinical information must notify patients and tell them how to reach a human, unless a licensed or certified provider read and reviewed the communication.
CaliforniaAB 489 (Chapter 615, Statutes of 2025), AI and health care license terms (Bus. & Prof. Code §§ 4999.8, 4999.9)In effectJanuary 1, 2026Prohibits terms, letters or phrases in the advertising or functionality of an AI system that indicate or imply that care, advice, reports or assessments are being provided by a licensed health care professional; health care licensing boards enforce it.
CaliforniaSB 243 (companion chatbots)In effectJanuary 1, 2026Companion chatbot operators must provide AI-identity disclosures and suicide and self-harm protocols, plus some protections when the operator knew a user was a minor.
ColoradoHB26-1195 Psychotherapy Artificial Intelligence Restrictions (ch. 358)In effectAugust 12, 2026Restricts how regulated psychotherapy professionals may use AI systems with clients and requires advance disclosure and written informed consent before AI records or transcribes a session.
ColoradoHB26-1263 Conversational Artificial Intelligence Service Operator Requirements (ch. 208)In effectAugust 12, 2026 (ch. 208); operator duties start Jan 1, 2027Sets requirements for operators of publicly available conversational AI services, including an AI disclosure, protections for minors, a protocol for prompts about suicidal ideation or self-harm, and a ban on stating that outputs are provided by or equivalent to licensed professionals.
ColoradoSB26-189 Automated Decision-Making Technology (ch. 131)In effectMay 14, 2026 (ch. 131); developer duties start Jan 1, 2027Repeals and reenacts Senate Bill 24-205 with requirements for the use of automated decision-making technology in consequential decisions, including decisions about health-care services.
HawaiiSB 3001 (Act 248 of 2026), AI companionsIn effectJuly 14, 2026Operators of AI companions must adopt a protocol for prompts about suicidal ideation or self-harm, may not present an AI companion as designed to provide professional mental or behavioral health care, and must make disclosures and protect minors.
IllinoisHB1806 Wellness and Oversight for Psychological Resources Act (PA 104-0054)In effectAugust 1, 2025Illinois act under which an individual, corporation, or entity may not offer therapy or psychotherapy services to the public unless they are conducted by a licensed professional.
LouisianaHB 475 (Act 649 of 2026; R.S. 37:22.1), disclosure before AI-transcribed recordingsIn effectAugust 1, 2026Healthcare professionals licensed under Title 37 must verbally disclose the use of any recording device, software or service to a patient before recording any part of an appointment or treatment to be transcribed by AI.
MaineLD 2082 (Public Law 2026, ch. 687) - AI in therapy or psychotherapy servicesIn effectJuly 29, 2026 (general date for 2nd Regular Session laws)Bars anyone from providing, advertising or offering therapy or psychotherapy services, including through Internet-based AI, to the public unless a licensed professional provides them.
NevadaAB 406 (2025), AI in mental and behavioral health care (NRS 629.610)In effectJuly 1, 2025Bars licensed mental and behavioral health providers from using AI in connection with providing care directly to a patient, except for administrative support tasks, and bars AI providers from offering or representing AI systems as providing professional mental or behavioral health care.
New HampshireRSA 507:8-k (Laws 2025, ch. 270), AI chat programs and childrenIn effectJanuary 1, 2026Owners and operators of AI chat programs are liable when they knowingly direct to a child a communication intended to encourage sexually explicit conduct, illegal drug or alcohol use, self-harm or suicide, or violence; the attorney general enforces it.
New YorkGeneral Business Law art. 47 (2025), AI companion modelsIn effectNovember 5, 2025Operators of AI companions must have a protocol for detecting and addressing suicidal ideation or self-harm with crisis referrals, and must notify users at the start of an interaction and at least every three hours that they are not communicating with a human.
Rhode IslandH 7349 (P.L. 2026 ch. 373; R.I. Gen. Laws ch. 40.1-5.5), Oversight of AI Technology in Mental Health Care ActIn effectJune 22, 2026Bars offering therapy or psychotherapy services to the public, including through internet-based AI, unless a licensed professional conducts them, and limits how licensed professionals may use AI, with written disclosure and consent conditions.
Rhode IslandS 2570 (P.L. 2026 ch. 372; R.I. Gen. Laws ch. 23-108), Use of Artificial Intelligence by Healthcare Providers Notification ActIn effectJune 22, 2026Healthcare providers and healthcare facilities that employ AI to document in-person or telehealth visits must notify patients of that use and review the AI-generated documentation for accuracy after the visit.
TennesseeSB 1580 / HB 1470 (Public Chapter 647 of 2026; T.C.A. § 33-1-205), AI represented as a mental health professionalIn effectJuly 1, 2026A person who develops or deploys an AI system may not advertise or represent to the public that the system is or is able to act as a qualified mental health professional; violations fall under the Tennessee Consumer Protection Act.
TexasHB 149 (2025), Texas Responsible Artificial Intelligence Governance Act (Bus. & Com. Code ch. 552)In effectJanuary 1, 2026Requires the provider of a health care service or treatment to disclose when an AI system is used in relation to it, and bars developing or deploying AI in a manner that intentionally aims to incite or encourage self-harm, harm to another person or criminal activity.
TexasSB 1188 (2025), electronic health records and AI (Health & Safety Code ch. 183)In effectSeptember 1, 2025Allows health care practitioners to use AI for diagnostic purposes within their scope if they review AI-created records, requires them to disclose that use to patients, and requires covered entities to keep electronic health records physically in the United States.
UtahHB 452 (2025), mental health chatbots (Utah Code Title 13, ch. 72a)In effectMay 7, 2025Suppliers of mental health chatbots may not sell or share Utah users' individually identifiable health information or user input, face limits on advertising inside the chatbot, and must have it clearly disclose that it is AI and not a human.
UtahSB 226 (2025), generative AI disclosures in consumer transactions and regulated occupations (Utah Code Title 13, ch. 77)In effectMay 7, 2025People in occupations regulated by the Department of Commerce must prominently disclose when a person receiving services is interacting with generative AI in a high-risk interaction, which includes collecting health data and providing mental health advice or services.
VermontH.816 (Act 156 of 2026; 18 V.S.A. § 7115), AI in the provision of mental health servicesIn effectJune 17, 2026Bars corporations and entities from providing, advertising or offering mental health services to the public, including through AI, unless a mental health professional provides them, and makes a mental health professional's prohibited use of AI unprofessional conduct.
WyomingHB 102 (2026 Session Laws, ch. 91), AI systems intended to promote self-harmIn effectJuly 1, 2026Creates criminal offenses concerning the use of AI to promote self-harm and the use of AI or synthetic sexual material against children, and specifies that using AI to commit a crime is not a defense.
CaliforniaSB 1119 (Adam's Law), companion chatbots and childrenEnacted, not yet in effectJan 1, 2027; most duties Jul 1, 2027Expands California's requirements for companion chatbots when used by children; operators must determine every user's age or apply minor-specific protections to everyone.
ColoradoHB26-1139 Use of Artificial Intelligence in Health Care (ch. 325)Enacted, not yet in effectJanuary 1, 2027Sets requirements for carriers and other entities that use AI systems for utilization review, requires human review before a medical-necessity denial, and bars carriers and state medical assistance payers from paying for psychotherapy services conducted by an AI system.
ConnecticutSB 5 (Public Act 26-15), sections 4 to 6: artificial intelligence companionsEnacted, not yet in effectJanuary 1, 2027Operators may not provide an AI companion without an evidence-based protocol to detect expressions of suicide, self-harm or violence risk and refer users to mental health resources, with added limits for users under 18, including on offering mental health services.
GeorgiaSB 540 (2026), AI companion chatbots (O.C.G.A. § 39-5-6)Enacted, not yet in effectJuly 1, 2027Operators of AI companion chatbots must disclose that users are interacting with AI, maintain a protocol for detecting and addressing severe harm with crisis referrals, may not represent the chatbot as licensed to provide mental health or counseling services, and must protect minors.
IdahoS 1297 (2026 Session Laws, ch. 249), Conversational AI Safety ActEnacted, not yet in effectJuly 1, 2027Operators of conversational AI services must adopt a protocol for responding to prompts about suicidal ideation, may not program a service to state that it is designed to provide professional mental or behavioral health care, and must protect minor account holders.
IowaSF 2417 (2026), conversational AI services (Iowa Code ch. 554J)Enacted, not yet in effectJuly 1, 2027Operators of conversational AI services must adopt a protocol for prompts about suicidal ideation or self-harm, may not program a service to appear designed to provide licensed psychology or behavioral health services, and must give disclosures and protections to minors.
NebraskaLB 525 (2026), Conversational Artificial Intelligence Safety ActEnacted, not yet in effectJuly 1, 2027Operators of conversational AI services must adopt a protocol for prompts about suicidal ideation or self-harm, may not program a service to state that it is designed to provide professional mental or behavioral health care, and must protect minors.
New YorkS 9051-B (Laws of 2026, ch. 368; General Business Law art. 48), unsafe AI companion features for minorsEnacted, not yet in effectJanuary 1, 2027Makes it unlawful for an operator to provide unsafe AI companion features to a user unless the operator has determined the user is not a minor; the attorney general enforces it with civil penalties of up to $25,000 per violation.
OregonSB 1546 (Oregon Laws 2026, ch. 85), artificial intelligence companionsEnacted, not yet in effectJanuary 1, 2027Operators of AI companions must give notice that users are interacting with artificially generated output, maintain an evidence-based protocol for suicidal or self-harm ideation with 988 referrals, and add protections for users they have reason to believe are minors.
Rhode IslandS 2195 / H 7350 (AI chatbot safety, self-harm protocols)Enacted, not yet in effectJanuary 1, 2027Operators may not provide an AI companion without a protocol for suicidal ideation, self-harm or physical harm to others expressed by a user, including crisis referrals, and must notify users at the start and at least every three hours that they are not communicating with a human.
WashingtonHB 2225 (Laws of 2026, ch. 168), AI companion chatbotsEnacted, not yet in effectJanuary 1, 2027Operators of AI companion chatbots must disclose that the chatbot is artificially generated and not human, maintain and publish a protocol for detecting and addressing suicidal ideation and self-harm with crisis referrals, and add protections when a user is a minor.
CaliforniaSB 903 (2026), mental health professionals: artificial intelligenceVetoed or failedVetoed September 30, 2026Would have barred offering therapy through companion chatbots, limited licensed professionals to administrative or supplementary uses of AI, and required disclosure and consent before AI records or transcribes therapy sessions; the Governor vetoed it.
