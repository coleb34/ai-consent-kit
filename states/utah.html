AI laws to know in Utah

LawStatusEffectiveWhat it covers
HB 452 (2025), mental health chatbots (Utah Code Title 13, ch. 72a)In effectMay 7, 2025Suppliers of mental health chatbots may not sell or share Utah users' individually identifiable health information or user input, face limits on advertising inside the chatbot, and must have it clearly disclose that it is AI and not a human.
SB 226 (2025), generative AI disclosures in consumer transactions and regulated occupations (Utah Code Title 13, ch. 77)In effectMay 7, 2025People in occupations regulated by the Department of Commerce must prominently disclose when a person receiving services is interacting with generative AI in a high-risk interaction, which includes collecting health data and providing mental health advice or services.

What this means for your practice

  • Comply with all requirements of your regulated occupation when providing services through generative AI. (effective May 7, 2025) Source
  • If a person receiving your services is interacting with generative AI in the provision of regulated services and that use is a high-risk AI interaction, prominently disclose it: verbally at the start of a verbal interaction and in writing before the start of a written interaction. (effective May 7, 2025) Source
  • If you supply a mental health chatbot, do not sell to or share with any third party a Utah user's individually identifiable health information or user input, except as the section allows. (effective May 7, 2025) Source
  • If you supply a mental health chatbot, have it clearly and conspicuously disclose that it is an AI technology and not a human: before the user may access its features, at the start of an interaction after seven days without access, and whenever the user asks. (effective May 7, 2025) Source
  • Know the enforcement: the Division of Consumer Protection administers and enforces the chapter, and its director may impose an administrative fine of up to $2,500 for each violation. (effective May 7, 2025) Source
  • Know the safe harbor: there is no enforcement action for a disclosure violation if the generative AI itself clearly and conspicuously discloses, at the outset of and throughout the interaction, that it is generative AI, is not human, or is an AI assistant. (effective May 7, 2025) Source
  • Know what counts as a high-risk AI interaction: an interaction with generative AI that involves collecting sensitive personal information, including health data, or providing personalized recommendations, advice or information such as mental health advice or services. (effective May 7, 2025) Source
  • Know who the disclosure rule covers: a regulated occupation is one that is regulated by the Department of Commerce and requires a license or state certification to practice. (effective May 7, 2025) Source

Good practice everywhere, whatever your state requires

  • Written consent from every adult participant before recording or transcribing a session.
  • A HIPAA business associate agreement for any AI tool that receives client information.
  • A clinician reviews every AI-assisted note.
  • AI never delivers therapy or makes clinical decisions.
