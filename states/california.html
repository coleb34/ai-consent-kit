AI laws to know in California

LawStatusEffectiveWhat it covers
AB 3030, generative AI in patient communications (Health & Safety Code § 1339.75)In effectJanuary 1, 2025Health facilities, clinics and physician offices that use generative AI to communicate patient clinical information must notify patients and tell them how to reach a human, unless a licensed or certified provider read and reviewed the communication.
AB 489 (Chapter 615, Statutes of 2025), AI and health care license terms (Bus. & Prof. Code §§ 4999.8, 4999.9)In effectJanuary 1, 2026Prohibits terms, letters or phrases in the advertising or functionality of an AI system that indicate or imply that care, advice, reports or assessments are being provided by a licensed health care professional; health care licensing boards enforce it.
SB 1119 (Adam's Law), companion chatbots and childrenEnacted, not yet in effectJan 1, 2027; most duties Jul 1, 2027Expands California's requirements for companion chatbots when used by children; operators must determine every user's age or apply minor-specific protections to everyone.
SB 243 (companion chatbots)In effectJanuary 1, 2026Companion chatbot operators must provide AI-identity disclosures and suicide and self-harm protocols, plus some protections when the operator knew a user was a minor.
SB 903 (2026), mental health professionals: artificial intelligenceVetoed or failedVetoed September 30, 2026Would have barred offering therapy through companion chatbots, limited licensed professionals to administrative or supplementary uses of AI, and required disclosure and consent before AI records or transcribes therapy sessions; the Governor vetoed it.

What this means for your practice

  • Board of Psychology licensees and registrants: per the Board's AB 489 advisory, make any use of AI in clinical, supervisory, educational or administrative settings transparent and appropriately disclosed, and never represent AI outputs as psychologist-provided judgment or services. (effective January 1, 2026) Source
  • Board of Psychology licensees: per the Board's AB 489 advisory, clearly identify AI-generated content, including documentation produced in whole or in part by AI, do not attribute it to yourself, and review, verify and validate any AI-generated material you use. (effective January 1, 2026) Source
  • Do not use AI to provide health information, advice or services while representing, or allowing it to be understood, that a licensed professional is delivering them when that is not so; this includes using protected titles or other representations of licensure. (effective January 1, 2026) Source
  • Do not use a term, letter or phrase in the advertising or functionality of an AI system that indicates or implies that care, advice, reports or assessments offered through the AI are provided by a natural person who holds a health care license. (effective January 1, 2026) Source
  • Get the consent of all parties before using a recording device to record a confidential communication, in person or by telephone or other device; recording one intentionally without the consent of all parties is a crime under Penal Code section 632. Source
  • In a health facility, clinic or physician's office (including a group practice) that uses generative AI to communicate patient clinical information, make sure patients are notified that generative AI created the communication, unless a licensed or certified human provider read and reviewed it. (effective January 1, 2025) Source
  • In those settings, give patients clear instructions on how to contact a human health care provider, employee or other appropriate person. (effective January 1, 2025) Source
  • In those settings, place the notification where the law specifies: at the beginning of each written communication, throughout a chat-based or video interaction, and verbally at the beginning and end of an audio communication. (effective January 1, 2025) Source
  • Know AB 3030's reach: a physician's office means an office of a physician in solo practice, and an office of a group practice means an office in which two or more physicians are legally organized as a partnership, professional corporation or licensed not-for-profit corporation. (effective January 1, 2025) Source

Good practice everywhere, whatever your state requires

  • Written consent from every adult participant before recording or transcribing a session.
  • A HIPAA business associate agreement for any AI tool that receives client information.
  • A clinician reviews every AI-assisted note.
  • AI never delivers therapy or makes clinical decisions.
