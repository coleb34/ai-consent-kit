What this means for your practice

  • Before using AI to assist in providing supplementary support, inform the client or the client's legally authorized representative in writing that AI will be used and of the specific purpose of the AI tool or system. (effective July 29, 2026) Source
  • Check your licensing board's rules on AI: the section requires the board to adopt rules to implement its requirements. (effective July 29, 2026) Source
  • Comply with all state and federal confidentiality and privacy laws when using AI, and ensure that any AI technology is compliant with those laws. (effective July 29, 2026) Source
  • Do not allow AI to make independent therapeutic decisions, directly interact with clients in any form of therapeutic communication, or generate therapeutic recommendations or treatment plans without your review and approval. (effective July 29, 2026) Source
  • Do not deny or refuse therapy or psychotherapy services to a client on the sole basis that the client has not consented to the use of AI to assist in providing supplementary support. (effective July 29, 2026) Source
  • Do not provide, advertise or otherwise offer therapy or psychotherapy services to the public, including through Internet-based AI, unless a licensed professional provides them; a violation is a violation of the Maine Unfair Trade Practices Act. (effective July 29, 2026) Source
  • Do not rely on a client's waiver of these protections: any waiver by a client of the section's provisions is contrary to public policy and is void and unenforceable. (effective July 29, 2026) Source
  • Get consent to the use of AI from the client or the client's legally authorized representative before using AI to assist in providing supplementary support. (effective July 29, 2026) Source
  • If you use an AI chatbot or other computer technology with consumers in a way that may mislead a reasonable consumer into believing they are engaging with a human being, notify the consumer in a clear and conspicuous manner that they are not engaging with a human being. Source
  • Know the consequence: a violation of the section is subject to disciplinary action by your board under Title 10, section 8003, subsection 5. (effective July 29, 2026) Source
  • Know the exception: the section does not apply to an AI-based intervention used solely within a research project approved by an institutional review board and conducted in compliance with all applicable federal protections for human subjects in research. (effective July 29, 2026) Source
  • Know who counts as a licensed professional: an individual with a valid Maine license or certificate to practice psychotherapy or behavioral health therapy, including under Title 32, chapters 31, 56, 81, 83 and 119, or a physician or physician associate who specializes in mental disorders. (effective July 29, 2026) Source
  • Make sure consent is a clear, explicit, affirmative act giving express, informed, voluntary, specific written agreement (electronic, or by initialing a specific section of the general consent to treatment) that is revocable; accepting broad terms of use is not consent. (effective July 29, 2026) Source
  • Tell the client in writing how session data collected by AI will be stored, retained, used for training and deleted upon termination of therapy or psychotherapy services. (effective July 29, 2026) Source
  • Use AI to assist in providing supplementary support, such as preparing client records and therapy notes, only when the client's therapeutic session is recorded or transcribed and only if the written-information and consent requirements are met. (effective July 29, 2026) Source
  • When AI assists with administrative or supplementary support, act within your scope of practice and standards of practice, and keep full responsibility for all interactions, outputs and data use associated with the use of AI. (effective July 29, 2026) Source

Good practice everywhere, whatever your state requires

  • Written consent from every adult participant before recording or transcribing a session.
  • A HIPAA business associate agreement for any AI tool that receives client information.
  • A clinician reviews every AI-assisted note.
  • AI never delivers therapy or makes clinical decisions.
