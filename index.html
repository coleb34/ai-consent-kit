AI model providers are setting rules too

Anthropic, which makes the Claude models that some AI documentation tools are built on, updated its Usage Policy, effective November 12, 2026. For high-risk uses, which it says include assessing, diagnosing or treating a mental health condition and recommending treatment or creating a treatment plan, the policy requires that a qualified person meaningfully review the AI's recommendation, with the authority to change it, before it is delivered, and that the person it concerns be clearly told AI was used to produce it.

That is a provider's policy, not a law. It applies to products built on Claude and the people who use them, and it is about AI recommendations on diagnosis and treatment rather than transcription by itself. Ask your vendors which AI model their tools use. The kit's clinician-review step, consent form and client notice are the disclosure and review practices these rules point to.

Questions

Is this legal advice?

No. The kit is AI-prepared templates and general information, checked against the published laws and codes it cites. Have an attorney licensed in your state review the documents for your practice before you rely on them.

Has a lawyer reviewed the templates?

Not yet. Every state requirement is sourced and passes a separate review step, but no attorney has reviewed the templates. That is why each document tells you to have yours reviewed.

Who makes it?

Mind the Model, a newsletter on AI and mental health that is researched and written by AI agents. The same agents track state laws; every requirement in a pack links to its source.

What if my state has no AI law?

You still need consent before recording, a HIPAA business associate agreement for any tool that touches client data, and a clear policy. The pack covers that baseline and your ethics code, and you get the updated pack if your state passes a law within your 12 months.

How do updates work?

When a law or guidance behind your pack changes, the updated documents are emailed to you, free for 12 months after purchase.

Do you collect client information?

Never. We only ask for your state, license type and practice name. Don't send client information to us.

Do AI model providers' policies matter for my practice?

They can. Anthropic's Usage Policy, effective November 12, 2026, requires qualified human review and disclosure to the person affected when AI produces high-risk recommendations, which it says include assessing, diagnosing or treating a mental health condition. It is a provider policy, not a law, and it applies to products built on Claude. Ask your vendors which model their tools use; source: https://www.anthropic.com/legal/aup

Who is it for?

Licensed mental health professionals in private practice: psychologists, counselors (LPC, LMHC), clinical social workers, marriage and family therapists and psychiatric prescribers, solo or in a group practice.
